[Usage](https://noket.dev/usage) shows what your account has called and what it cost. What you can see
depends on your role:

- A model owner can view their own usage or the usage of all their models.
- An admin sees their own usage and that of the members they manage.
- A member sees only their own usage.

The server enforces this, so filters can only narrow what you already have access to.

## Time range

Choose **Today**, **7 days**, or **30 days**, and optionally narrow to a model. The range changes what
is counted; it does not reset any budget. Budgets reset on their own periods, shown on the model.

## Key figures

The top of the page summarizes the selected range:

- Cost
- Requests
- Tokens
- Success rate
- Cache hit rate
- Average latency
- End-to-end throughput
- Cost per request

Below it, the page charts daily activity, token trends stacked by input, cache, and output, the
composition of tokens and of cost, and the outcome of requests.

## Per-model table

The table at the bottom has one row per model: requests, token breakdown, cost, cache hits, average
latency, data status, and a trend.

- **Average latency** counts only requests that reported a latency, divided by the number of valid
  requests.
- **Unknown** and **partial** data are labelled. A figure the gateway has not reported yet is marked
  provisional rather than shown as zero.

## How the numbers are counted

- Days follow the **Asia/Singapore** time zone, so a day boundary is not your local midnight.
- Cost comes from the per-request cost reported by the gateway and is used for budget control. It is not
  necessarily what a provider will invoice you, and it is not an amount anyone owes.
- All amounts are in USD.
- Usage is tracked per model. Two models backed by the same upstream account still have separate
  totals and separate limits.
- Filtering by time does not change budget counters. A budget period resets on its own schedule.
